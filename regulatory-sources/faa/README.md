## Regulatory Chain

The FAA repair-station framework should be analysed through:

1. Title 49, United States Code
2. Title 14, Code of Federal Regulations
3. FAA Orders and policy
4. Advisory Circulars and guidance
5. Repair Station Certificate
6. Operations Specifications
7. Repair Station Manual and Quality Control Manual
8. Training programme
9. Maintenance records and objective evidence

## Related Regulations

Applicability must be determined separately for each organisation and activity:

- 14 CFR Part 1 — Definitions and abbreviations
- 14 CFR Part 21 — Certification procedures for products and articles
- 14 CFR Part 39 — Airworthiness Directives
- 14 CFR Part 91 — General operating and flight rules
- 14 CFR Part 119 — Certification of air carriers and commercial operators
- 14 CFR Part 121 — Domestic, flag and supplemental operations
- 14 CFR Part 135 — Commuter and on-demand operations
- 14 CFR Part 5 — Safety Management Systems

Do not assume that every requirement in these Parts applies to every certificated repair station.

### Advisory Circulars

Relevant Advisory Circulars may address:

- Repair Station Manual
- Quality Control Manual
- Training programmes
- Human Factors
- Maintenance records
- Contract maintenance
- Safety Management Systems

Each Advisory Circular must be registered separately with its exact number, revision and current status.

## Approval and Scope Evidence

The following documents should be controlled separately from the regulation:

- Repair Station Certificate
- Operations Specifications
- Ratings and limitations
- Approved locations
- Repair Station Manual
- Quality Control Manual
- Training programme
- Capability list
- Contract-maintenance controls

A certificate confirms approval status, while Operations Specifications and related approval records establish the approved scope and limitations.

## Audit Evidence

Typical evidence includes:

- Personnel rosters and qualification records
- Inspection and supervisory authorisations
- Training records
- Tool and calibration records
- Current maintenance data
- Work packages and release records
- Contract-maintenance records
- Internal audit and corrective-action records
- FAA surveillance and correspondence

## Source-Control Note

Do not treat an FAA Order or Advisory Circular as a mandatory regulation unless its legal effect is established through an applicable regulation, approval condition or incorporated requirement.

Do not conclude compliance solely because a certificate is valid. Scope, ratings, limitations, locations, procedures and implementation evidence must be checked separately.
